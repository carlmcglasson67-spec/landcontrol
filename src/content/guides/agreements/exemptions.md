## The six exemptions

| Exemption | What it covers | The edge |
|---|---|---|
| Short-term | Rights that can only last less than 18 months | Count every extension. If it could reach 18 months, it is not exempt |
| Not for development | Rights held exclusively for a purpose unrelated to housing or development of 100 m²+ floorspace | “Exclusively” does the work. Investment options may still be caught |
| Overage | Rights securing further payments to a former owner | Only the overage security, not other rights in the same deed |
| Finance security | Rights necessary or incidental to security for a loan or financial instrument | Must be security, not a commercial option dressed up |
| Section 106 | Rights relating exclusively to infrastructure, amenities or services linked to planning | Affordable housing rights are not exempt |
| National security or defence | Rights in contracts made for these purposes | Narrow by design |

## Short-term rights

Practice guide 84 section 3.2.1 exempts rights that can last less than 18 months, and says the exemption fails if the contract allows 18 months or more. HMLR’s scenario 5 is a 12-month option with a 12-month extension: potentially reportable, because it could run for 24 months. Over time, an older right can drift *into* this exemption: an option with less than 18 months left when it is assigned may not need reporting.

## Not for development

The exemption in section 3.2.2 covers rights held exclusively for a purpose not relating to housing, or to development creating 100 square metres or more of floorspace. HMLR tells readers to interpret it narrowly. Two scenarios show how narrow:

- **Investment options (scenario 6):** where the investment depends on the land rising in value, which may be linked to future development, the exemption may not apply.
- **Golden brick contracts (scenario 7):** a registered social landlord’s right to homes not yet built depends on development after the contract date, so the exemption does not apply and the RSL may have to report.

If you rely on this exemption, write down the purpose at the time and keep the evidence.

## Overage and finance security

Rights securing overage payments to a former landowner (section 3.2.3), and rights necessary or incidental to security for a loan or financial instrument (section 3.2.4), are exempt. Both protect money, not control. A contract can mix them with other rights: an option in the same document is assessed on its own.

## Section 106

Section 3.2.5 exempts rights relating exclusively to infrastructure, amenities or services linked to planning permission. HMLR’s scenario 8 draws the line: a right concerning the use of flats as affordable housing is not infrastructure, amenities or services, so the exemption is not available.

## Things that are never in scope

Separately from the exemptions, some rights fail the basic tests:

- statutory rights and court-ordered rights (not a written contract);
- options over an easement or other right rather than the land;
- contracts for leases of 15 years or less, and transfers of leases with 15 years or less left;
- rights over land that is wholly unregistered;
- rights held for purely personal purposes, until assigned to a business;
- rights granted by someone who was not yet the registered owner, until something else triggers reporting.
