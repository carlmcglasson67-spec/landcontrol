## The offence

Part 11 of the Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023 gives the power to require contractual control information, and section 225 makes it an offence to:

- **fail to provide** the information required, without reasonable excuse; or
- **knowingly or recklessly provide false or misleading** information.

The MHCLG guidance confirms that offences can apply to individuals, partnerships and corporate bodies, and to officers or employees acting for them. The duty is the grantee’s, so the exposure sits with the developer or promoter and its officers. An indemnity from the landowner does not move it.

The published guidance does not state fine levels. Treat the offence as a board-level risk rather than a line item.

## Reasonable excuse

HMLR has given a useful steer. Its scenarios guidance says that if you follow that guidance and Practice guide 84 and, on that basis, reasonably conclude that information is not required in a particular scenario, you may consider that this amounts to a reasonable excuse. Two practical points follow:

1. **Record your reasoning.** For every right you decide is exempt or out of scope, keep a note of which test it failed and why, dated at the time.
2. **Recheck when things change.** A variation or assignment can bring an exempt right back in.

## The commercial consequence

This is the one most land teams will feel first. Practice guide 84 says HMLR will not generally enter a notice or restriction protecting a contractual control right until all the required information has been provided, and will not generally accept the application while it is outstanding. HMLR will ask applicants whether the entry relates to a contractual control right and whether the Regulations have been complied with.

Without a notice or restriction, an option or conditional contract may not bind a buyer of the land. If the landowner sells to someone else, your remedy may be a damages claim against them rather than the site.

## Getting it wrong, not just missing it

The offence covers false or misleading information given knowingly or recklessly, not only silence. Practice guide 84 does not yet describe a correction process; the only stated route is the contractual controls team at HMLR. Build checking into the process: the conveyancer submits, but the grantee is accountable for material errors or omissions.

## How to stay clear

- Put a contractual control step in every completion, variation and exercise checklist.
- Use one register of rights with an owner, a status and a deadline for each.
- Diary 6 October 2027 for transitional rights now.
