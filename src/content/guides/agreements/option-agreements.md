## What counts as an option

Practice guide 84 describes the first category of contractual control right as an option that lets the holder make the owner transfer or lease land. In practice that means the classic strategic land option: the developer pays a premium, gets an exclusive period to promote the site through planning, and can call for a transfer at a price fixed by formula once permission is granted.

An option is caught only if every one of the general tests is met:

- it is in a **written contract** (a deed counts);
- it controls a future **transfer of the freehold, or grant of a lease of more than 15 years**, of **registered land**;
- the grantor is the **registered owner** when it is granted;
- it is held **for the purposes of an undertaking**, such as a business; and
- **no exemption** applies.

Options over only an easement or other right, rather than the land itself, are outside the regime.

## The 18-month question

The exemption that matters most for options is the short-term one. A right is exempt if it can only last for less than 18 months. HMLR reads that strictly: you count every extension the contract allows. Its scenario 5 is a 12-month option with an option to extend for a further 12 months. Because it could last 24 months, the exemption does not apply and the option may be reportable.

> If any route through the contract gets the option to 18 months, assume it is caught.

The flip side helps with older options. HMLR’s guide gives the example of an option granted in 2025 and assigned in 2028: by the time of the assignment, it may have become short-term, in which case it may not need reporting at all.

## Lease options

An option to take a lease is caught only if that lease would be for more than 15 years. HMLR’s scenario 3 is a 12-year lease with an option to take a further lease. It is not reportable: the relevant term is the lease the option relates to, and the existing term and the further term are not added together.

## Investment options

An option held as an investment is not automatically outside the regime. The exemption for rights not held for development applies only if the right is held exclusively for a purpose unrelated to housing or development creating 100 square metres or more of floorspace. HMLR’s scenario 6 suggests that where the value of an investment option depends on future development, the exemption may not apply.

## What you report, and when

| Event | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Option granted 8 Jun 2026 to 5 Apr 2027 | Before 6 October 2027 |
| Option granted on or after 6 Apr 2027 | 60 days from grant |
| Assigned, or varied in writing (e.g. extended) | 60 days, even for options granted before 8 Jun 2026 |
| Exercised, determined or expired | 60 days |

For an option, HMLR asks for the exercise date or the conditions for exercise, the **initial period of control** (from the contract date to the latest exercise date, ignoring extensions), then any **extension** provisions and either party’s right to **terminate**. Work out a date with the [deadline calculator](/deadline-calculator/).

## What the market will see

From April 2028, the public dataset will show who holds the option, the land, the type of right and how long it runs. Expect adjoining owners and competitors to know when your option expires. The confidentiality clause in the option agreement binds the landowner; it does not stop HMLR publishing.
