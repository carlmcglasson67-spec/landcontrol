## Why pre-emptions are in scope

A pre-emption right freezes the market for a site: no other buyer gets a clean shot while it exists. Practice guide 84 lists it as one of the four categories of contractual control right, alongside options, conditional contracts and rights to direct or request a sale.

The general tests apply in full. The right must be in writing, over registered land, granted by the registered owner, relating to a future transfer of the freehold or a lease of more than 15 years, held for the purposes of an undertaking, and not exempt.

## Where pre-emptions sit in real deals

Pre-emption rights turn up in more places than standalone agreements:

- as a **fallback** in a promotion agreement or option, for example a right of first refusal that bites if the option lapses;
- in **joint venture** arrangements over adjoining land;
- on a **sale of part**, where the seller keeps a right of first refusal over the land sold, or the buyer takes one over land retained.

HMLR’s scenarios guidance confirms that each right in a contract is assessed separately. A pre-emption that sits inside a larger agreement may need its own report even if the main right is exempt or already reported.

## What is different about the information

The information required is the same as for any right (see [what HMLR will ask for](/hmlr/information-required/)) with one simplification: you do **not** need to give the date from which a pre-emption can be exercised or describe the conditions. You still give the initial period of control, any extension provisions and any termination rights.

## When it ends

For a pre-emption, Practice guide 84 treats the right as exercised when the right of pre-emption itself is exercised. The grantee then has 60 days to confirm the ending. Expiry or release of the right is also an ending and is reported the same way.
