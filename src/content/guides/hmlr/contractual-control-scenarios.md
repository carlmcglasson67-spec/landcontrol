## How to read them

HMLR is explicit that the scenarios reflect its **current understanding**, do not override the Regulations, and that it is not ultimately responsible for interpreting them. They matter anyway, for a practical reason: HMLR says that if you follow this guidance and Practice guide 84 and reasonably conclude that information is not required, you may consider that a reasonable excuse. That makes the scenarios the benchmark for any decision not to report.

## The nine

| # | Scenario | Outcome | The rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contract with someone not yet the registered owner | Not reportable on grant | Grantor must be the registered proprietor. Becomes reportable on a notice/restriction application, or if varied or assigned after the seller is registered |
| 2 | Option held for a family home | Not reportable while held personally | Must be held for an undertaking. Assign it to a builder and the builder must report |
| 3 | 12-year lease with an option for a further lease | Not reportable | Judge the term of the lease the option relates to. Terms are not added together |
| 4 | Several rights in one contract | Each assessed separately | Information for every right that meets the criteria |
| 5 | 12-month option, extendable by 12 months | May be reportable | Short-term exemption counts extensions. 24 months possible, so no exemption |
| 6 | Option bought as an investment | May be reportable | If the value depends on development, the non-development exemption may not apply |
| 7 | Golden brick contract | Potentially reportable, possibly by the RSL | The RSL’s use depends on development after the contract, so no exemption |
| 8 | Section 106 affordable housing right | Infrastructure exemption doesn’t apply | Affordable housing is not infrastructure, amenities or services |
| 9 | Right granted after 8 June 2026, varied January 2027 | Reportable before 6 October 2027 | Cannot file before 6 April 2027. Must reflect the variation and be accurate when filed. A notice application may make it due sooner |

## What land teams should take from them

**Exemptions are narrow.** Three of the nine (5, 6 and 8) close off exemptions people expected to rely on. If your position depends on an exemption, document why.

**Look at each right, not each deal.** Scenario 4 confirms what most practitioners assumed: one promotion agreement can generate several reports.

**Some rights wake up later.** Scenarios 1 and 2 describe rights that are dormant at grant and become reportable when something happens: the seller completes, the right is assigned to a business, someone applies for a notice. Your register needs to track those triggers, not just the reportable rights.

**Housing associations are in scope.** Scenario 7 puts registered social landlords on golden brick deals in the frame as grantees.
