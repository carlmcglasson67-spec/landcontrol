## The four clocks

Practice guide 84 sets out the events that start a 60-day reporting period. Each runs from the date of the event.

| Event | What you report | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Grant on or after 6 April 2027 | The full contractual control information | PG 84 §4.1 |
| Assignment to a new grantee | The change of grantee | PG 84 §4.1, §4.3 |
| Written variation that alters the information | The nature of the change and when it took effect | PG 84 §4.1, §4.3 |
| Right ends: exercise, determination or expiry | How it ended, and whether wholly or partly | PG 84 §7.2 |

HM Land Registry’s note for legal professionals describes the period as **60 calendar days**. Practice guide 84 does not set out a day-counting rule, so treat the 60th day as the last possible day and aim to file well before it. The [deadline calculator](/deadline-calculator/) counts it for you and exports the date to your calendar.

## What does not start a clock

- **A variation that doesn’t change the reportable information.** Only a variation in writing that alters information already required is a trigger. A change to, say, the price formula that does not affect the period, the land, the type of right or the exercise conditions is not.
- **Events before 6 April 2027.** Assignments, variations and endings only become triggers from the day the Regulations come into force.
- **Events affecting rights that are outside the regime.** An exempt right stays exempt, though a variation can bring it in (for example, an extension taking a short option past 18 months).

## The fifth trigger: notices and restrictions

If you apply for a notice or restriction to protect a contractual control right, HMLR will generally not accept the application until the information has been provided. This applies whenever the right was granted, including before 8 June 2026, and in practice it can bring the deadline forward to the day you want to protect the right.

## When events overlap

Deals move. A right might be assigned and then varied within a few weeks. Practice guide 84 section 4.3 allows one report to cover several events if it is updated before the deadline, and if a right ends within the 60 days after an assignment or variation, you can confirm the ending instead.

## Who is on the hook

The duty sits with the **grantee**. The submission has to go through an [individually regulated conveyancer](/hmlr/who-can-submit/). Build the 60 days into your acquisitions process: every completion checklist, every deed of variation and every option exercise notice should carry a contractual control step.
