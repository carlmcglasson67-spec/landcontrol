## What will be published

The MHCLG guidance lists the fields that will be in the public dataset:

- **where the land is and its extent**;
- **who holds the right** (the grantee);
- **the type of right** and **how long it runs**;
- **when it was granted or exercised**.

It will be published in a standardised, downloadable format and updated at least monthly. Publication starts as soon as possible after 6 April 2028.

## What stays private

- dates and places of birth of individual grantors;
- conveyancers’ professional registration numbers;
- prices, premiums and the commercial terms of the deal, which are not part of the information required in the first place.

## What the first release will contain

The first release will be built from everything reported since the service opened on 6 April 2027: the transitional backlog of rights granted from 8 June 2026, every grant since, and every older agreement brought in by an assignment or variation. It will not be a full picture of the land market on day one. Options signed before June 2026 that have not changed will be missing, and will appear gradually as they are assigned or varied.

## Who will use it, and how

**Developers and promoters** will see competitors’ pipelines: who is working where, at what scale, and when their control expires. A rival’s option running out next spring is a lead.

**Landowners** approached by a promoter will be able to see who controls the neighbouring land, and for how long, before they negotiate.

**Planning authorities** will be able to see who controls sites in their area when preparing local plans and housing land supply.

**Lenders, valuers and analysts** will be able to check control rights over a title as part of due diligence, and track land-bank activity by group.

## The catch with raw data

A monthly download of rights against title numbers is a starting point, not an answer.
