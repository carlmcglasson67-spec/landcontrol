## What the guide covers

Practice guide 84 is aimed primarily at solicitors and other conveyancers, and HMLR says it often deals with complex matters in legal terms. It sits alongside a separate [scenarios guidance](/hmlr/contractual-control-scenarios/) published the same day. The guide covers the agreements and land in scope, when information must be provided, how and by whom, and what information and supporting material is needed.

## Scope (section 3)

Four kinds of right are covered: an **option** to make the owner transfer or lease land; a **conditional contract**; a **right of pre-emption**; and a legal **right to direct or request a sale or lease**, often found in promotion agreements.

Each must be in a written contract (a deed counts), control a future transfer or long lease by the registered owner, and be held for the purposes of an undertaking. Generally only the primary right is covered; a secondary right is covered only if it is protected by a notice or restriction.

## Exemptions (section 3.2)

Short-term rights (under 18 months), rights held exclusively for purposes other than housing or 100 m²+ floorspace development, overage, finance security, section 106 infrastructure, and national security or defence. Full detail in [exemptions](/agreements/exemptions/).

## When to report (section 4)

- **Grant on or after 6 April 2027:** within 60 days.
- **Grant from 8 June 2026 to 5 April 2027:** before 6 October 2027. Only the grant is a trigger in this period, but the submission must reflect later changes.
- **Grant before 8 June 2026:** not reportable on grant; reportable when assigned or varied on or after 6 April 2027.
- **Assignment or written variation that alters the information:** within 60 days.
- **Notice or restriction application:** information first, whatever the grant date.
- **Overlapping events:** one report can cover several if updated before the deadline.

## How to submit (sections 5 and 6)

A new service, **Report contractual control over land**, launches in the HMLR Portal from 6 April 2027, with Business Gateway to follow. Technical guidance is promised before launch. The grantee is responsible; an [individually regulated conveyancer](/hmlr/who-can-submit/) must submit. One conveyancer can provide information for several rights under one contract.

## What to provide (section 7)

Parties and identifiers, type of right, the contract, exercise date or conditions, initial period of control, extensions, termination, title numbers, location, address and UPRN, and sub-surface or airspace. When a right ends, how it ended and whether wholly or partly. Field by field in [what HMLR will ask for](/hmlr/information-required/).

## Consequences (section 9)

It is an offence to fail to provide required information without a reasonable excuse, and HMLR will not generally enter a notice or restriction until the information is provided. See [penalties and consequences](/compliance/penalties-and-consequences/).

## What it leaves open

As published, the guide does not cover fees, a day-counting rule, spatial tolerances, a correction process or penalty levels, and the exact questions in the digital service are not yet confirmed. We track each of these on the [implementation tracker](/tracker/) and will update this page when HMLR does.
